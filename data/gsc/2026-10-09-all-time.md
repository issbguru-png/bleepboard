## Follow-up, 2026-10-10: per-page query filters

### The /about/ and /categories/ "anomaly" is sitelinks. No action.

Both pages looked broken: top-three average positions converting at ~1.4%.
Filtering by page shows why, and it is not a defect.

/about/      7 clicks / 1,346 impressions across exactly 4 queries:
               bleepboard   5 cl / 1,129 im / pos 1.8
               bleep board  2 cl /   189 im / pos 4.2
               bleepbord    0 cl /    22 im / pos 5.8
               bleep bord   0 cl /     6 im / pos 6.3
/categories/ 3 clicks / 757 impressions, 727 of them from "bleepboard" at pos 1.8

Both are sitelinks under the brand search. Google shows a cluster of our
pages for "bleepboard", every one of them books an impression, and the
visitor clicks the homepage, which is the correct outcome. Reading those
impressions as independent rankings was the error. Page-level impression
totals inflate for brand queries generally, so treat them with care.

### Keyword cannibalisation: blog posts outranking their own hubs

query                 impressions  position  page Google actually serves
italian brainrot          68         63.8    /blog/italian-brainrot-explained/
goofy ahh                 25         28.8    /blog/goofy-ahh-meaning-sounds/
animal sound buttons      19         25.3    /animal-soundboard/   (correct page)

Two of three go to a blog post rather than the hub covering the same term.
/brainrot-soundboard/ sits at position 12.4 and holds 35 sounds, yet the
blog post is what ranks for "italian brainrot". /goofy-ahh-soundboard/ holds
11 sounds and does not rank for "goofy ahh" at all.

This matters because of the conversion gap: hubs run 9.3% CTR, blog pages
0.32%. Google is routing these queries to the format that cannot convert
them. Fixing CTR on the blog post treats the symptom; the disease is that
the wrong page is eligible.
