Long before it was a meme, "tung tung tung" was a real sound doing a real job. During Ramadan in Indonesia, someone walks the streets before dawn beating a *kentongan*, a hollow wooden slit drum, so the whole neighbourhood wakes in time to eat before the day's fast begins. In early 2025 that wake-up call acquired a face, a baseball bat and a few billion views, and adults everywhere started typing the same confused question into Google.

## What does tung tung tung sahur mean in English?

The phrase splits cleanly in two. **Sahur** is the pre-dawn meal Muslims eat before fasting through the daylight hours of Ramadan. **Tung tung tung** means nothing on its own; it's onomatopoeia for the drum that wakes you up for that meal, the Indonesian equivalent of writing "knock knock knock".

So the nearest honest translation is roughly *"bang, bang, bang, time to get up and eat"*. Not a curse, not a code, not a hidden reference. The menace arrived later, and it was added deliberately.

## Who created Tung Tung Tung Sahur?

The character comes from an Indonesian TikTok creator posting as **Noxa** (@noxaasht), who uploaded the first video on **28 February 2025**, the day before Ramadan began in Indonesia. The design is an anthropomorphic kentongan, a tall wooden drum given a face and a bat, generated with AI image tools. People who describe it as a haunted log are not far off, but the log is specifically the drum from the tradition, which is the detail that makes the whole thing cohere.

The original Indonesian voiceover plays it as mock horror. It introduces the creature as an anomaly that only appears at sahur time, and warns that if you're called to wake up three times and stay in bed, it comes to your house. Indonesian viewers got the joke instantly: it's a monster built out of being told to get up.

## Is Tung Tung Tung Sahur real?

Half of it is. The tradition is completely real. Kentongan drumming for sahur happens across Indonesia every single Ramadan, and has for generations. The character is not. There's no ancient legend of a wooden creature punishing heavy sleepers, whatever the more confident lore videos claim. It's a 2025 invention wearing a genuine tradition as a costume, and the costume is the best thing about it.

## The song and the "lyrics"

Most people asking about the tung tung tung sahur song mean the audio from the original video and the wave of remixes that followed. The lyrics barely exist: the name itself, chanted over the drum rhythm, plus that Indonesian warning about sleeping through the third call. There's a reason it lodges in your skull so firmly. This rhythm was engineered, centuries before TikTok, to drag unwilling people out of sleep. Your brain refusing to let go of it means the drum is working as designed.

## Why an Indonesian meme joined Italian brainrot

Here's the odd part. The character is Indonesian in origin, language and subject, yet it became one of the flagship figures of [Italian brainrot](/blog/italian-brainrot-explained/), the wave of AI-generated absurdity that produced [Tralalero Tralala](/sound/tralalero-tralala/) and [Bombardiro Crocodilo](/sound/bombardiro-crocodilo/). The canon didn't care about geography. An uncanny AI character with a rhythmic nonsense-sounding name and deadpan fake lore ticked every box the genre had, so the community adopted it on sight, and Triple T (as fans call it) ended up carrying a real Ramadan tradition to millions of teenagers who had no idea they were learning something.

The Roblox game *Steal a Brainrot* then did for the character what TikTok couldn't: it put Tung Tung Tung Sahur in front of kids too young for the For You page, as one of the collectable characters. For a lot of nine-year-olds, the game came first and the meme second.
