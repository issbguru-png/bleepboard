If you've spent any time on TikTok or YouTube Shorts lately, you've heard it. A man screaming **"GET OUT"** with a fury that borders on unhinged, slapped onto the end of videos as the ultimate ejection notice. That scream has a name, a face, and one of television's greatest origin stories.

## Where the sound comes from

The clip is **Tuco Salamanca**, the volatile drug kingpin from the early seasons of *Breaking Bad*, played by Raymond Cruz. Tuco is one of television's most memorably unstable villains, a man whose mood can flip from celebration to violence inside a single breath, and the "GET OUT" scream catches him at full boil, ordering someone out of his presence with no room whatsoever for negotiation.

The delivery is what makes it perfect meme material. This isn't an annoyed "get out" or a firm "get out". It's a total, throat-shredding eviction of another human being from a space. There's no comeback to it. The conversation, the visit and arguably the relationship are all over.

## How it became a meme

Like a lot of great meme sounds, the clip sat quietly in the culture for years before short-form video weaponised it. TikTok editors started deploying the scream as a **punchline ejector seat**. Build up any scenario at all, an opinion nobody asked for, a player doing something cursed in a game, an animal misbehaving, then hit the GET OUT.

The format works because the scream is wildly disproportionate to anything you attach it to. A friend suggests pineapple pizza? *GET OUT.* Someone's phone goes off in the cinema? *GET OUT.* The gap between the tiny offence and Tuco's thermonuclear response is the joke, and it hasn't stopped being funny yet.

Search interest followed the trend. "Get out meme" and "get out sound effect" are now among the most-searched meme sound queries anywhere, and the clip has settled into the permanent meme canon alongside the Vine Boom and the Wilhelm Scream.

## How people use it

- **As a reply.** Sending the sound in a group chat or Discord call is a complete response to any bad take.
- **In edits.** The ejection punchline: build-up, offence, GET OUT, cut to black.
- **In games.** Timed with kicking someone from a lobby or party, which may well be its highest purpose.
- **As a prank.** Played at full volume behind someone mid-sentence.
