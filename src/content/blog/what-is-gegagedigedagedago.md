Nobody agrees on how to spell gegagedigedagedago, which is inconvenient for the enormous number of people trying to Google it. Ge-ga-ge-di-ge-da-ge-da-go is the version that stuck, more or less, and it refers to one of the strangest audio memes TikTok has produced: a nonsense chant, sung with total commitment, usually coming out of a chicken nugget.

## It's Cotton Eye Joe. Sort of.

Strip the meme back and there's a recognisable song under it: **"Cotton Eye Joe"** by Rednex, the 1994 Eurodance-does-country track that has haunted school discos ever since. The gibberish sits exactly where the chorus goes. Sing "where did you come from, where did you go" fast enough, with enough slurring, and you can hear how a run of syllables like *gegagedigedagedago* falls out of it.

That mishearing didn't happen by accident. In July 2023 a TikToker called **razioff** posted a cover of the song with most of the actual words replaced by confident gibberish, sung straight. The cover was funny on its own and picked up tens of millions of plays. But audio memes need a body, and this one found the strangest body available.

## Then the nugget arrived

Separately, in August 2023, an account called **wenonugget** had started posting videos of a chicken nugget with a face on it, animated to sing. Not any face: the blank, wide-eyed **Man Face from Roblox**, deepfake-animated so the nugget's features move with the vocal. The first nugget performance was James Brown. In January 2024 the two memes collided when the nugget sang razioff's gibberish cover, and by February the combination was everywhere.

That fusion is the meme most people mean today: Roblox-faced nugget, mouth flapping, belting *gegagedigedagedago* like it's the last night of a farewell tour. From there it did what successful TikTok audio does, detaching from the nugget and landing on pets, babies, footballers and anything else that could be made to mouth along. The remixes went everywhere too. Metal versions, medieval bardcore versions, versions where an entire choir of nuggets performs in harmony. The syllables survived every genre unharmed, since there were no words to mistranslate in the first place.

A note on attribution, since reuploads have scrubbed most of it: the singing is a person, razioff, not a text-to-speech engine. The AI part is the animation that makes the nugget's face move. Plenty of explainers call the whole thing "an AI song", and that's wrong in an interesting way; the machine supplied the mouth, but a human supplied the commitment.

## Why nonsense syllables stick

The chant works for the same reason toddlers' songs work. The syllables are rhythmically regular, every vowel is open, and there's nothing to understand, so nothing gets in the way of repeating it. Your brain files it as a real word because it behaves like one. It belongs to the same family of meaning-free audio as [the 67 meme](/blog/what-does-67-mean/): content you cannot explain to an adult because there is genuinely nothing to explain, and the shared secret of knowing it is the entire reward.

The meme also handed Rednex the strangest career boost in Eurodance history, sending a thirty-year-old song rocketing back up streaming and YouTube charts because millions of teenagers wanted to find out what the nugget was mispronouncing.
